## How to use this book

**Read it in order the first time.** The chapters build. Chapter 5 assumes you met the enrollment process in Chapter 3, and Chapter 10 assumes you know what a decision table is. After that, treat it as a reference and jump.

**The running example is a university course-registration system.** Students, courses, sections, prerequisites, enrollment, the registrar. It is the only example in the book. When a chapter needs a data model, it models registration. When it needs a screen, it designs the enrollment screen.

**Watch for R-01.** It first appears in Chapter 2 as a requirement — a student can't enroll without the prerequisite — and it reappears in six later chapters in six different forms. Following it is the fastest way to understand what traceability means.

**Every chapter ends with review questions.** They aren't graded and there are no answers in the back. Several have no single right answer; they ask you to argue a position and say what would change your mind. That's deliberate. Answers are available to instructors.

**Every chapter also carries a case study.** These describe an analysis situation and what it is designed to teach. Your instructor may set them as work, adapt them, or ignore them.

**The figures are diagrams, not decoration.** Forty-eight of them, all produced with the same notation you're being asked to use. If a figure and a paragraph seem to disagree, trust the figure and tell someone.
