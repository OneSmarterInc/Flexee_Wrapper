# Preface

This book is written for business students. You may be studying accounting, finance, marketing, management or another business discipline. This may be the only course you take in management information systems. I have written it with that possibility in mind.

My aim is to give you concepts you can use wherever your work takes you: how information changes a process, how a number can mislead, how a useful technology can become a poor investment, and how responsibility remains when a system helps make a decision.

These questions belong throughout a business. A marketer needs to understand what customer data permits the company to infer. An accountant needs to know how a transaction became a reported number. An operations manager needs to understand why an order stopped moving. An entrepreneur needs to judge what a new service will cost and whether people can actually use it.

Each is a question about technology. Each is also a question about people, work and consequences.

The book begins with business situations because a concept is easier to understand when you can see why someone needs it. Technical terms appear when they help explain the situation. The questions at the ends of chapters ask you to use what you have read: examine an assumption, work through a small example or recommend a decision.

I have kept the book short deliberately. An introductory course should give you a foundation you can remember and apply. There is more to learn about every subject here. The purpose is to help you recognise what matters, know when you need specialist advice and ask questions that make that advice useful.

Technology will be part of the decisions you encounter. My hope is that you approach those decisions with curiosity, a willingness to examine evidence and a clear sense of responsibility for what follows.
